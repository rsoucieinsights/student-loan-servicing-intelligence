Primary analytical boundary

Volume is a signal.
It is not a rate.

CFPB complaint counts can identify concentration and change. They cannot establish which servicer performs worst without a compatible exposure denominator.

The decision-ready story

The topline barely moved. Payment trouble did.

As 7.5 million borrowers entered a repayment-plan transition, payment-handling complaints posted one of their sharpest increases since 2024—concentrated at MOHELA. The timing creates a credible early-warning hypothesis, not causal proof.

FSA explains the operating environment.

Federal portfolio and FY2024 Q4 servicer-performance files provide structural context: portfolio scale, repayment status, and differences in repayment-plan mix.

  • Useful for interpreting the environment around complaint patterns
  • Useful for identifying plausible portfolio-mix explanations
  • Useful for defining the exposure data a fair comparison would need

Why it is not the denominator

The time, population, and measurement units do not align.

The servicer-performance extract is FY2024 Q4; the complaint analysis extends through July 2026. Its portfolio fields also do not represent the same complaint-eligible exposure for every company and month.

Complaint counts÷FY2024 Q4 context≠servicer rate

Governance

The limitations are product features.

Each control prevents a plausible analytical mistake and keeps the dashboard suitable for early warning rather than unsupported ranking.

Complete periods

August 2026 is partial and excluded from complete-month comparisons.

Narrative lag

Recent public narratives may appear later; low coverage blocks current theme conclusions.

Taxonomy drift

Issue choices and company names can change, so definitions and crosswalks are versioned.

Nonrepresentative data

Complaints reflect awareness and willingness to report, not population prevalence.

Theme overlap

Transparent keyword themes may coexist; their shares must not be summed.

Complaint-ID lineage

Derived tables retain complaint IDs so findings can be traced to source records.

Product roadmap

Automate the repetition. Keep judgment at the release gate.

  • Monthly KPIs, contribution analysis, and candidate theme flags
  • Draft dashboard and executive briefing
HUMAN REVIEW

Analytical release

  • Join and taxonomy-change assessment
  • Narrative interpretation and alternative explanations
  • Evidence label, headline calibration, and escalation decision
  • Final privacy, traceability, and executive QA
FAIRER RATES REQUIRE

Compatible exposure

  • Monthly borrowers or serviced accounts by servicer
  • Portfolio transfers and company-name crosswalks
  • Comparable product, status, and repayment-plan composition
  • A documented numerator–denominator eligibility rule
